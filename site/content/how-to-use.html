{{APP}} gets deleted photos, videos and files back from memory cards, USB sticks and external drives. There's nothing to set up and no account to create. Here's the whole process, from plugging in the card to having your photos back on your Mac.

Before you start: stop using the card. Don't take new photos on it or copy anything onto it, because new files can land on top of the ones you want back.

  1. Download and open {{APP}}

    Download {{APP}}, open the file, and drag {{APP}} into your Applications folder. It's signed and notarized by Apple, so it opens like any other app. It needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later, and works on Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

  2. Plug in the card and click Scan

    Put the card in your Mac's SD slot or a card reader, or plug in the USB stick or drive. It appears in the list by itself, named after the camera that used it. Click it to start.

    macOS then asks for your Mac's password. That's macOS's own password box, and it lets macOS open the card read-only for {{APP}}. You're asked once per scan. The very first time, macOS may also ask you to allow access to removable volumes, and {{APP}} shows you where to click.

  3. Watch your files appear

    Deleted files usually show up within seconds, with their original names and grouped by the day they were taken. A deeper search then reads every part of the card to find anything else, and new finds keep appearing while you look.

    The card disappears from Finder during the scan. That's on purpose: {{APP}} keeps macOS from writing its own files onto the card until you're done.

  4. Pick the ones you want

    Click photos to select them, or use Select next to a day to take the whole day. The sidebar narrows the list to photos, RAW files, videos or documents, and the search box finds files by name. Double-click a file, or press Space, to see it full size before you recover it. Videos play right there.

  5. Recover them to your Mac

    Click Recover. {{APP}} suggests a dated folder inside Pictures, or you can choose another place on your Mac. It won't save anything back onto the card you're recovering from.

    When it's done, click Show in Finder to see your photos. That's it.

Good to know

  • Nothing on the card changes. {{APP}} only reads it, so you can scan as many times as you like.
  • Files marked Partial or Damaged were partly overwritten by newer files. They may open with missing pieces, or not at all. Use the View Options menu to hide damaged files.
  • Files without their names were found by the deep search after their record was gone. {{APP}} names them by the date and time they were taken, plus the camera model.
  • A card that's struggling can be copied once with Save a Copy of This Disk, from the menu on its card. You can then scan the copy without touching the card again.
  • Deleted from your Mac itself? The Mac's own drive can't be scanned for deleted files. Our guide to files deleted from a Mac shows where to look instead.

Questions

Do I need to wait for the scan to finish?

No. Files with their names usually appear within seconds, and you can recover them straight away. Leave the deep search running if something you're looking for hasn't appeared yet.

Why does {{APP}} ask for my password?

macOS only lets apps read a card's raw contents with an administrator's permission. The password goes to macOS, not to {{APP}}, and it only lets {{APP}} read that one disk.

Where do the recovered files go?

To a folder on your Mac. {{APP}} suggests a dated folder inside Pictures › {{APP}}, and you can pick any other folder that isn't on the card you're recovering from.

Can I stop and come back later?

Yes. Stop the scan whenever you like, and scan the card again later. Because nothing on the card was changed, you'll find the same files again.
