It started with a Fujifilm card

We needed to rescue deleted Fujifilm photos from an SD card. So we did what most people do. We searched, downloaded a recovery app and ran a scan. It found the photos. Then it asked for money before it would save a single one.

We tried another app, and another. Each one scanned for free, then wanted a subscription or a license fee before it would save the files. Some offered a free allowance measured in megabytes, which a few RAW files use up.

There's real work in recovery software, and we don't begrudge anyone charging for it. But it felt wrong to find our photos and then be asked to pay to get them back, at a moment when most people are anxious and short of options.

So we built our own

Camera cards use the FAT32 and exFAT file systems, and a deleted photo usually stays on the card until something is saved over it. Getting it back takes careful reading of the card and a lot of testing, and once ours worked, it seemed silly to put it behind a paywall for the next person in the same position.

{{APP}} grew from there. It now reads deleted file records to bring files back with their original names, runs a deep scan for formatted cards, handles RAW files from all the major camera brands, and works with USB sticks and external drives as well as memory cards.
