What "free" usually means in Mac recovery apps

Most recovery apps for Mac follow the same pattern. The download and the scan are free, and the app shows you what it found. Saving the files is where the limit applies. Depending on the app, the free version saves nothing at all, a set number of megabytes, or a set number of files. A few hundred megabytes covers a handful of RAW photos, or less than a minute of 4K video from many cameras.

That's a reasonable way to sell software, and the free scan does tell you whether your files are there. It's worth knowing the limit before you spend an hour scanning, though.

What the paid tools do that {{APP}} doesn't

To be fair to them, paid recovery apps cover things {{APP}} deliberately doesn't.

  • Repairing damaged files. Several paid tools offer photo or video repair. {{APP}} recovers files as they are on the disk and marks the ones that are Partial or Damaged.
  • File names on more file systems. {{APP}} reads file records only on FAT32 and exFAT. On NTFS, APFS and Mac OS Extended drives it relies on its deep scan, so files come back without their names. Some paid tools can read more of those file systems' records.
  • Windows versions and support teams. {{APP}} is Mac only, made by a small company.
  • Scanning your Mac's internal drive. Some paid tools offer this, after you lower your Mac's security settings. {{APP}} doesn't, because TRIM and encryption mean there's rarely anything left to find. Here's where to look instead.

How to choose

  • Photos or videos deleted from a camera card or USB stick: start with {{APP}}. If it finds your files, you're done, for free.
  • A rare file type: try PhotoRec, which recognizes far more formats. Our PhotoRec on Mac guide walks through it.
  • Files on an NTFS or APFS drive where names matter, or damaged videos that need repair: a paid tool may be worth it. Run its free scan first and check the previews.

Before you pay for any recovery app

  • Run the free scan and check that the files you need appear, with previews that look right.
  • Check the free version handles your device. EaseUS's free Mac edition, for example, doesn't support SD cards.
  • Read whether the price is a subscription that renews automatically, and what the refund policy says.
  • Whatever you use, recover to a different disk from the one you're recovering, and stop using the card or drive until you're done.
